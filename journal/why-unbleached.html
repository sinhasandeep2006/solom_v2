Materials

Why our tissue isn’t white

Most tissue starts out the colour of the pulp it’s made from: somewhere between oatmeal and light tan. The bright white on a supermarket shelf is added later, in a bleaching step.

What bleaching is for

Bleaching is mostly about looks. It makes a sheet read as “clean” at a glance, but it doesn’t make it softer or stronger. In a lot of paper, those qualities come from the fibre itself.

Why bamboo lets us skip it

Bamboo fibre is long. Long fibres knit together into a sheet that holds up when it’s damp, so it doesn’t need extra treatment to feel soft. We keep the sheet as it comes and let the colour be what it is.

What that means at home

A warm-toned tissue with no added scent or dye. The first time you use one it looks a little different. After a week you stop noticing, except that nobody’s nose is sore by the end of a cold.
